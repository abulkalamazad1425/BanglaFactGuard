What is Headline Alteration?

It checks whether the claim headline says the same thing as the source report's title. Only the title is compared — never the article body — because the title is the statement the outlet stands behind.

What do “matched” and “altered” mean?

Matched: the headline is the same as the title, or has the same meaning. Altered: a name, number, date, negation, attribution or the main statement differs. If neither can be established, no verdict is given.

What does “Source not found” mean?

The search found no matching report in the selected outlet. It is not proof that the claim is false — the report may be worded differently, removed or not yet indexed.

What are the body similarity scores?

TF-IDF cosine similarity
How much the two bodies share their important words.
Jaccard similarity
Words used by both ÷ all distinct words used by either.
Normalized Levenshtein similarity
1 − character edits ÷ length of the longer text.
Semantic similarity (LaBSE)
Meaning-based closeness from a multilingual embedding model (embedding cosine, not BERTScore).

How should I read a score?

Scores run from 0 (nothing in common) to 1 (identical). High is 75% or more, moderate 40–74%, low below 40%.

Why are body scores not a verdict?

Similar wording does not prove truth, and one changed number can hide in very similar text. The scores never decide the headline verdict.

When is a body score unavailable?

When no body was submitted, no source report was found, the source text could not be read, or that measurement failed. It is shown as “Unavailable”, never 0%.

How is a photo card read?

Gemini reads the headline, date and outlet from the image (up to three attempts). If that fails, EasyOCR is used. Only the headline is verified; the outlet and date you selected are used.

How is the publication date checked?

Your claimed date is compared with the report's publication date (Bangladesh time). A mismatch alone does not mean the news is false.
